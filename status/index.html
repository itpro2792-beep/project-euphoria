Privacy boundary

Local-first is not secret

The Toolbox remains local-first, not private-by-design, while it shares the https://itpro2792-beep.github.io origin. Its entries use unencrypted browser storage scoped to that whole origin—not only to the Toolbox path. Current or future same-origin code, the same browser profile, or a sufficiently privileged extension could potentially read them. Clearing site data can erase them.

A dedicated hostname will be called private-by-design only after origin isolation, response headers, storage scope, third-party code, update paths, backup behavior, deletion, and recovery have been reviewed and tested. A dedicated hostname alone would not encrypt the device or protect data from browser-profile access or privileged extensions.

The current meta-delivered content security policy blocks scripted network connections from the Toolbox page. HTTP response headers are preferred and remain part of the dedicated-origin gate. GitHub Pages also records visitor IP addresses for security purposes; that hosting behavior is outside the Toolbox’s JavaScript. GitHub’s Pages documentation explains that collection.

Public outcome receipts

None published yet

The separate Receipts page records public source history, corrections, bounded evidence-record zeroes, and pending work. Those are build and claim-accounting receipts—not evidence that a household practice benefits people.

Nothing in a private Ledger becomes a public receipt automatically. A receipt may be published only after every identifiable person represented gives specific, informed, voluntary consent to the exact wording and audience, and the item passes redaction and evidence review. Consent may be withdrawn for future display, but deletion from screenshots, archives, or copies already made cannot be promised.

Wins, failures, and pending outcomes remain separate. One receipt never proves effectiveness. A publishable record must state its claim, date or period, evidence class, outcome, scope and limits, consent state, redaction review, and last review version.

Identity and claim boundary

Robbie in the room; Siren in the system record

Robbie is the room-facing conversational name. Siren is the software system and operational identity. Status pages, permissions, versions, incidents, and evidence use “Siren.” Conversational copy may use “Robbie,” but it does not imply a second entity, an undisclosed human, or a capability that has not been verified.

Machine consciousness remains an open philosophical question, not a roadmap deliverable or product claim. Voice, memory, self-description, relational language, and fluent conversation do not establish sentience. Project Euphoria evaluates inspectable behavior—consent, provenance, accuracy, reliability, and recovery—and labels unknowns as unknown.
