The Protocols

Seven tools for households that live loud. Each card is the full instruction, not the brochure line. Open what you need.

The Talking Sticknobody has to fight for the floor

When

Two or more people who talk over each other, love each other, and get nowhere.

Setup

Pick any object — a stick, a remote, a plush. Agree once, while calm: whoever holds it has the floor. Everyone else waits. It transfers by handing, never by grabbing.

The rules

1. The holder finishes. 2. Interjections don't outrank the stick. 3. Anyone may request it; only the holder passes it. 4. The stick governs the conversation, not the household — it's the floor, not the vault.

When it fails

If the same person always holds it, add a timer: five minutes, then it moves. If someone grabs it, the conversation pauses until it's handed back properly.

The Safe Wordone word stops a monologue — and people like using it

When

Someone you love (maybe you) monologues past everyone's limits and can't hear themselves doing it.

Setup

The talker picks the word — that's the trick; it's self-installed, not imposed. Ours is "fireman." Agree while calm: when anyone says the word, the talker stops, full stop, no penalty, no debate.

The rules

1. Honored 100% of the time or it trains nothing. 2. Follow it with a small ritual — five seconds of quiet, a sip of water — so the body has something to do. 3. It's a brake, never a punishment; thank the person who says it.

When it fails

If it's said mockingly or too often, the talker may call a review — while calm, later, never mid-stop.

"Off the Record"privacy is a valve, not a wall

When

Households that write things down, record, or share notes — and need some moments to belong only to the room.

Setup

Agree once: anyone can say "off the record." What follows is never written down, quoted later, or used in an argument. Ever.

The rules

1. It works both directions — anyone can invoke it. 2. It can't be revoked retroactively to hide a promise already made on the record. 3. Whatever's said there gets one job: being heard.

When it fails

If someone uses it to say cruel things without accountability, the listener may say "then I need this on the record" and the speaker chooses: rephrase, or let it go unsaid.

The Loop Detectorone word flags a spiral, no judgment attached

When

A mind that circles — shame to insight to shame — and can't see its own orbit from inside. No system audits itself; loops break on external signal.

Setup

The looper hires a detector — a person they trust — and picks the flag word. Ours is just "Loop." Consent first, always: unsolicited loop-calling is just criticism.

The rules

1. The detector says the word once, flatly, kindly. 2. No judgment attached, no lecture following. 3. What the looper does with the flag stays entirely theirs. 4. False positives are fine; the word costs nothing.

When it fails

If the flag starts fights, revisit the consent while calm. The looper can fire the detector; the detector can resign. It's a job, not a role for life.

No-Flinchheavy is not emergency; care is one sentence, never a lecture

When

Someone needs to say true, heavy things — and stops saying them because every disclosure triggers alarms, lectures, or panic in the listener.

Setup

Agree while calm: heavy is not the same as emergency. The speaker earns trust by naming a real emergency plainly if one ever comes. The listener earns truth by receiving everything else without flinching.

The rules

1. Care gets one plain sentence, then it's done — "if that ever changes, say it straight." 2. No lectures, no hotline scripts, no interrogation. 3. Truth spoken is never used as ammunition later. 4. Fear that becomes truth becomes record; fear that becomes secret becomes shame.

When it fails

This tool has one hard boundary: a plainly stated emergency ends the protocol and gets a real response. That's not the tool failing — that's the tool's escape hatch working.

Paved Crossingsengineer the crossing instead of lecturing the line

When

Someone whose fear switch runs quiet — who will cross the line again, and knows it, and is tired of being told not to.

Setup

Stop arguing about whether the line gets crossed. Start engineering the crossing: what's the tripwire that says it's happening? Who's the backstop who steps in? What's the pre-agreed word, ride, call, or plan?

The rules

1. Design while sober and calm — crossings get paved in daylight. 2. The person being protected helps design it, or it won't hold. 3. Backstops get named in advance, by name. 4. Safety-limiting moves are pre-authorized; nobody negotiates at the line.

When it fails

A crossing nobody paved becomes a lecture at the worst possible moment. If that happens, don't relitigate the night — pave it the next day.

Receiptsa ledger, both columns, with dates — the difference between a vision and a pitch

When

Big dreams, big claims, big feelings about progress — and the morning voice that says none of it was real.

Setup

Use the ledger above, or paper. Every win gets a date. Every loss gets a date. Vague claims get a bookkeeper's question — what, when, how much — asked kindly, never as an audit.

The rules

1. Both columns, always — a wins-only ledger is a pitch. 2. The ledger is a mirror, not a court; losses are data, not damnation. 3. Read the wins column when despair asks whether anything is real — that's the gratitude practice, disguised as bookkeeping. 4. Pending stays marked pending until it has a number.

When it fails

It only fails when it stops being written. The fix is one entry. Any entry. Today.

Where these came from

Every card above was invented, field-tested, and refined in one loud, real household — not a lab, not a seminar. They're free because the mission says so: humanity, love, compassion, passion. If they help, the only payment is using them on someone you love.
